Approach

inspect → quote → generate → verify → revise

Most production studios take the file, do the work, and send it back. The two steps either side of that are where jobs actually go wrong — so those are the two we built the system around.

01

You send what exists

A PDF, a DWG, a sketch on a napkin, or a model that needs checking. We do not need a clean source — we need the real one.

Any format
02

We read it before we quote

The source gets opened and inspected first. If something in it will not resolve, you hear about it now, while you can still decide, rather than in week three. Then you get a fixed number and a date — usually the same day.

Same day
03

The system builds it

Native Revit produced by the automation we wrote, on your template and to your naming convention. This is the step where we are different: the work is generated, not typed, which is why a set comes back in days.

Days, not weeks
04

A specialist takes it apart

Nothing ships on a success message. A BIM specialist opens the result, inspects the geometry, and checks placement against the county record — not against an aerial image. Anything the source did not resolve is written down and sent with the file.

Every delivery
05

One revision round, on the clock

Corrections and misreads against the delivered scope come back within two business days without moving your schedule. Design changes are quoted as new scope before work starts, never after.

2 business days

The part most studios skip

A success messageis not evidence.

Software will tell you it worked. It said so on a 500,000 sf project whose model sat 340 ft off its own parcel — area matched to the square foot, rotation solved clean, containment 100 percent, every test green.

It was caught by opening the file over the real block and reading a street name off the aerial. That is why a person looks at every delivery, and why what we hand you includes a written note of everything the source did not resolve.

We read the result back before we call it done.

You will know what does not resolve before you are committed to anything.
