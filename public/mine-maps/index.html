Facts and limits

What the 3D views are built from

  • The mining company's own survey sheets, from public state archives (PA DEP / PASDA, WA DNR).
  • Placement from printed coordinate grids, streets, rail and streams. In a blind test on six gridded sheets, every one landed within 50 m of the state's own placement and five within 20 m (6–22 m).
  • Seam depth from floor elevations written on the sheets, under USGS 3DEP lidar terrain.
  • Parcels, buildings and addresses from county GIS and assessment records.

What they are not

  • Not a geotechnical report, property disclosure or engineering opinion.
  • Old maps can be incomplete: workings after the survey date, robbed pillars and unmapped entries may exist.
  • Where a view simplifies (for example a seam drawn as a flat or tilted plane), the view says so.
  • Absence of a mapped mine is not evidence that no mine exists.

Required before any decision

Before buying, designing, permitting or building on a site near mapped workings, the actual conditions must be confirmed by a physical site investigation: borings and/or geophysical survey, carried out and signed by a licensed professional engineer or geologist. Some jurisdictions require it by code; in King County, Washington, a coal mine hazard assessment by a Washington-licensed professional is required before permits in mapped hazard areas.
